# Regulatory Guidebook — Chapter 1: Chemical Classification Lists by Country

**Review date:** 4 November 2026  
**Coverage:** GHS/CLP workplace/supply classification of chemical substances and mixtures.  
**Status:** Practical quick reference, not a complete legal opinion. Always check the version in force on the assessment date.

## How to interpret the status

- **MANDATORY:** Published substance classifications are legally binding within the stated regulatory scope.
- **CONDITIONAL:** Legally binding for designated substances, approvals, hazards, or a particular scheme; not a complete general harmonised list.
- **ADVISORY:** Published substance classifications may support an assessment but are not generally mandatory.
- **NO GENERAL LIST:** Classification under local rules may be compulsory, but no general, nationally binding substance classification catalogue has been identified in the cited framework.

**Important:** A legally mandatory GHS classification *process* is not the same thing as a legally mandatory *list of classifications*.

## Practical instructions for SDS and mixture classification

1. **Establish the destination market and applicable legal regime** before selecting a classification source. For the UK, distinguish GB from Northern Ireland.
2. **Search by substance identity** (CAS/EC and chemical description) including salts, isomers, form, composition, impurities, and any index-number/group entries.
3. **Apply mandatory national entries where required**, but **only for the hazards specified**. Evaluate unlisted hazard classes using the locally applicable criteria.
4. **Check entry-specific details** such as specific concentration limits (SCLs), acute toxicity estimates (ATEs), M-factors, notes and adaptation/entry-into-force dates; these can change mixture classifications materially.
5. **Separate official decisions from other database content.** For example, the ECHA C&L Inventory contains notifier-submitted classifications alongside harmonised ones; being in a database does not itself make an entry mandatory.
6. **Record provenance:** issuing authority, exact list/entry, version or amendment, date checked, applicable hazard classes, and any expert departure/justification.
7. **Do not treat chemical inventories, restricted-substance lists, occupational exposure-limit lists, transport dangerous-goods tables or mandatory SDS coverage lists as harmonised GHS classification lists.** They serve different legal purposes.

## Regulatory caveats

- This is a deliberately **selective international overview**, not a determination that countries not mentioned have no relevant laws or lists.
- **Mandatory** does not mean all hazard classes of every listed substance are predetermined.
- **Conditional** jurisdictions require an entry-specific review before deciding the legally binding status of the classification.
- In non-harmonised jurisdictions, a supplier still has a legal duty to derive and justify appropriate hazard classifications.
- Local language and permitted GHS edition, as well as product-specific exemptions, can alter SDS and labelling obligations.
